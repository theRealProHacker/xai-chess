How to read the last move (from the ICS lessons, reduced to what a commentator checks)

After every move, answer two questions.

1. What does the mover threaten now?
   - Mate or check against the king; winning material; a piece landing on a strong square;
     taking an open file, rank or diagonal. Use the facts: a threat is real only if a listed
     capture wins material or the hanging section names the piece. Say which threats are real.

2. What did the move change? Every move has a plus and a minus.
   - It opened or blocked a file or diagonal; name the piece that gains or loses the line.
   - It left a square or pawn without its protector; name the square and who can use it.
   - It moved a defender away; name what is now undefended.
   - A pawn move is permanent: it no longer guards two squares and can no longer guard them
     later; name those squares and whether the opponent can occupy them.
   - It spent a tempo; say what the mover did not do instead (develop, castle, defend).

If neither question yields something concrete, judge the pieces, not the position.
   - Mobility: how many useful squares the moved piece has now, and which own pawns block it.
   - Placement: a knight in the centre and a rook or bishop on an open line are well placed;
     a piece behind its own pawns is not.
   - Role: out of play, only defending, attacking, or attacking and defending at once.
   - Stability: can the opponent drive it away with a pawn or a cheaper piece?
   - A "bad bishop" is one whose own pawns stand on its colour.

Other things worth a sentence when the facts show them.
   - Material and local superiority: who has more pieces near the target (a king or a weak pawn).
   - King safety: which pawns still shield the king, whether the castled king has a flight square.
   - Structure: isolated, doubled, backward or passed pawns, and holes no pawn can ever guard.
   - Initiative: whether the opponent must answer a threat or is free to improve.
   - Blockade and obstruction: a piece planted in front of a pawn, or a pawn shutting an enemy
     piece out.

Write only what applies to this move and this board.
