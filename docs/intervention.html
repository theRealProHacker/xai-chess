Intervention

Would the side to move rather pass?

How it works

The tempo operator (nullzug) compares Stockfish’s evaluation after the best move with its evaluation after a null move, where the side to move passes. If passing would be better, the position is zugzwang.

Play one move. Did it remove a forced mate?

How it works

Load a position with the side you want to defend to move and play exactly one move. The threat operator asks a narrow, mate-only question: did the opponent have a forced checkmate before your move — found by letting you pass (a null move) — and is that mate gone after it? Search only: no board edits and no engine patch (a Tier 1, confound-free test).

Switch one pin off and see whether the edge survives.

How it works

Set up a position where the side to move pins an enemy piece. Detected pins are arrowed; to pick another, right-drag an arrow from the pinning piece along the pin (needs a mouse; draw it after any moves). The engine evaluates the position twice on a patched Stockfish 18: once normally, and once with that one pin switched off at the rules level, nothing else on the board changed.

An absolute pin (to the king) frees the pinned piece to move; a relative pin (to a more valuable piece) forbids the pinner from capturing that backstop, which frees the pinned piece the same way. If the side to move’s edge — a forced mate, or a winning evaluation — exists only while the pin holds, the pin is load-bearing. On a quiet position the eval swing is reproducible at fixed depth, not a measured causal magnitude.

Block a line and see whether the position rests on it.

How it works

Mark the line an explanation credits (right-drag an arrow) and the engine drops a blocker on it: a phantom that cuts the ray for the pieces that use it — rooks and queens on a rank or file, bishops and queens on a diagonal — and leaves everything else alone. The square stays open; pawns, knights and kings pass through it as before.

It then drops the same blocker on squares off the line. Those set the noise floor. Up to three squares on the line are tried, and as many off it. A line earns the credit only if every square tried on it moves the evaluation further than an off-line square does. One square doing all the work is that square’s doing, not the line’s.
